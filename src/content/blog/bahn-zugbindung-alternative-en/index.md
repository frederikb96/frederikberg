In short

- **Two triggers.** The train binding is lifted as soon as DB announces an arrival at the destination at least 20 minutes late, or a booked change that can no longer be made.
- **The announcement counts.** What decides is what the app, bahn.de or the station display show at that moment. If the train catches up afterwards, the binding stays lifted.
- **Announced before departure:** the whole trip can be taken on another day, according to DB up to a year later.
- **Announced only on the way:** the ticket is valid from the station where the traveller gets off. The part already travelled is not travelled again.
- **A screenshot taken at the moment of the announcement** is the best proof, because the forecast disappears once the trains move on.

</div>

A Sparpreis or Super Sparpreis, Deutsche Bahn's discounted fares, is valid only on the long-distance trains it was booked for (*Zugbindung*, train binding). The binding falls away when the journey is disrupted, and the ticket then works on other trains too. Three questions follow:

- What exactly lifts the train binding?
- What applies if the situation improves again afterwards?
- Is the ticket then valid for the whole trip, or only for the rest of it?

The answers follow from the wording of DB's conditions of carriage in the edition of 26 October 2026, the EU rail passenger rights regulation and DB's help pages, all quoted verbatim below in the original German with a working translation.

## 1. What lifts the train binding

For a journey within Germany, each of these cases is enough on its own:

- **The arrival at the destination is expected at least 20 minutes late.** For international journeys the threshold is 60 minutes.
- **A booked change can no longer be made.** Whether a faster train exists does not matter.
- **A train or a stop is cancelled**, or a train leaves early because of a timetable change.

The basis is clause 9.1.1 of the conditions of carriage:

> Muss vernünftigerweise davon ausgegangen werden, dass der Reisende aufgrund einer Verspätung, eines verpassten Anschlusses oder eines Zugausfalls am Zielbahnhof gemäß Beförderungsvertrag mindestens 20 Minuten verspätet ankommen wird, hat er, auch mit einer zuggebundenen Fahrkarte, gegenüber dem EVU, dass die verspätete oder ausgefallene Beförderung vertraglich schuldet, unverzüglich die Wahl zwischen (i) der Fortsetzung der Fahrt oder der Weiterreise mit geänderter Streckenführung bis zum Zielbahnhof bei nächster Gelegenheit oder (ii) der Fortsetzung der Fahrt oder der Weiterreise mit geänderter Streckenführung bis zum Zielbahnhof zu einem späteren Zeitpunkt. Er kann dabei auch den Zug einer höherwertigen Produktklasse benutzen. Die Benutzung eines reservierungspflichtigen Zuges oder eines Sonderzuges ist allerdings nicht gestattet.
>
> *— Beförderungsbedingungen der DB AG, edition of 26 October 2026, BB Personenverkehr clause 9.1.1 — https://www.bahn.de/agb*

**Working translation:** where it must reasonably be assumed that the traveller will arrive at the destination station named in the contract of carriage at least 20 minutes late because of a delay, a missed connection or a cancellation, the traveller has, even with a train-bound ticket, an immediate choice, towards the rail undertaking that owes the delayed or cancelled service, between continuing the journey at the next opportunity, possibly by a different route, and continuing it at a later time. A train of a higher product class may be used. Reservation-compulsory trains and special trains may not.

DB's help page lists the cases one by one, the missed connection explicitly as a case of its own:

> Bei Tickets mit Zugbindung (Sparpreisen) ist diese Zugbindung in folgenden Fällen aufgehoben:
> - Bei erwartbarer Verspätung am Zielort von:
>   - mindestens 20 Minuten bei nationalen Reisen
>   - mindestens 60 Minuten bei internationalen Reisen
> - Bei Zugausfall
> - Bei Haltausfall
> - Wenn Sie Ihren Anschlusszug nicht erreichen
> - Wenn es durch eine Fahrplanänderung zu einer verfrühten Abfahrt des Zuges kommt
>
> *— bahn.de, „In welchen Fällen ist die Zugbindung aufgehoben und was bedeutet das?" — https://www.bahn.de/faq/zugbindung-aufgehoben-bedeutung*

**Working translation:** the train binding of Sparpreis tickets is lifted for an expected delay at the destination of at least 20 minutes on national and 60 minutes on international journeys, for a cancelled train, for a cancelled stop, when the connecting train is missed, and when a timetable change makes a train leave early.

On a line with hourly trains both triggers usually coincide: when a change breaks, the next connection runs an hour later, and the 20-minute threshold is met as well.

## 2. The forecast counts, not how the journey turns out

Both triggers are expectations, not outcomes. It follows that:

- **What counts is what DB announces**: in the app, on bahn.de, on the displays and in the announcements at stations and on trains.
- **The right arises at the moment the disruption is announced**, and it stays, even if the train later catches up and the connection would have been reachable after all.
- **There is no duty to check the situation again later.**
- **Proof is a screenshot from that moment**, a DB notification about the journey with its time, or a certificate. Within Germany a certificate is not required.

Clause 9.1.4 defines when the expectation is well-founded:

> Der Reisende kann insbesondere dann vernünftigerweise mit einer Verspätung nach Nr. 9.1.1 und Nr. 9.1.3 am Zielbahnhof rechnen, wenn diese über mindestens einen der nachfolgenden Informationskanäle bekanntgemacht wurde: (i) Aushangfahrpläne und ausgehängte Informationen über Fahrplanänderungen in Bahnhöfen, (ii) elektronische Anzeigen und Lautsprecheransagen in Zügen und auf Bahnhöfen, (iii) Fahrplaninformationen aus Buchungssystemen personalbedienter Verkaufsstellen sowie (iv) verfügbare Fahrplaninformations- und Reisendeninformationsmedien, insbesondere das Fahrplanauskunftssystem im Internet unter www.bahn.de. Die Übergangszeiten für planmäßige Umstiege (Umsteigezeiten) orientieren sich an der elektronischen Fahrplanauskunft unter www.bahn.de. Das Gleiche gilt, wenn der Reisende eine vom Beförderer oder vom Bahnhofsbetreiber ausgestellte Bestätigung vorlegen kann, aus der sich eine Verspätung nach Nr. 9.1.1 und Nr. 9.1.3 ergibt.
>
> *— Beförderungsbedingungen der DB AG, edition of 26 October 2026, BB Personenverkehr clause 9.1.4 — https://www.bahn.de/agb*

**Working translation:** the traveller may reasonably expect a delay under clauses 9.1.1 and 9.1.3 in particular where it has been announced through at least one of the following channels: posted timetables and posted information about timetable changes at stations; electronic displays and announcements on trains and at stations; timetable information from the booking systems of staffed sales points; and available timetable and passenger information media, in particular the online timetable information service at www.bahn.de. Transfer times for scheduled connections follow that same electronic timetable information service. The same applies where the traveller can present a confirmation issued by the carrier or the station operator showing such a delay.

DB thereby makes its own information service the yardstick, and the transfer times stored there the yardstick for whether a change still works. Clause 9.1.1 grants the choice *unverzüglich*, immediately, at the moment the disruption becomes known, and the clause contains no condition under which it lapses again. DB's help page supports this with the sentence „Hinweis: Bei Fahrkarten mit Zugbindung (z. B. Sparpreisen) ist diese automatisch aufgehoben." (*Working translation:* for tickets with train binding, such as Sparpreis fares, the binding is lifted automatically; https://www.bahn.de/faq/zugbindung-aufgehoben-bedeutung). A validity of up to a year also sits poorly with a right that could vanish again the same evening.

A duty to check again would hardly be possible to meet in practice. Once an alternative offered in the DB Navigator app is adopted, the app no longer shows the original connection. The selection itself changes nothing about the ticket:

> Wichtig: Wenn Sie eine alternative Verbindung auswählen, behalten Sie Ihr ursprüngliches Ticket. Es werden ausschließlich die angezeigten Informationen im Reiseplan und die Benachrichtigungen zur Reise angepasst. Bitte prüfen Sie vorher, ob Ihr Ticket für die ausgewählte Alternative gültig ist.
>
> *— bahn.de, DB Navigator, Funktionen: „Mein Zug ist verspätet, wie kann ich in der App eine alternative Verbindung auswählen?" — https://www.bahn.de/faq/pk/service/buchung/navigator/funktionen*

**Working translation:** important: when you select an alternative connection, you keep your original ticket. Only the information shown in the travel plan and the notifications for the journey are adjusted. Please check beforehand whether your ticket is valid for the selected alternative.

Against this reading it can be argued that a forecast must hold at the moment of the decision, and that a forecast visibly overtaken by events no longer carries. That is true for the moment of the choice itself, but it does not extend to a duty to keep watching afterwards: clause 9.1.1 describes a point in time, and clause 9.1.4 provides for no later comparison.

On proof, the help page says:

> Innerhalb Deutschlands benötigen Sie keine zusätzliche Bescheinigung zur Aufhebung der Zugbindung. Sie können sich aber auf expliziten Wunsch eine Bescheinigung ausstellen lassen (z.B. im Zug, im Reisezentrum oder an der DB Information).
>
> *— bahn.de, „In welchen Fällen ist die Zugbindung aufgehoben und was bedeutet das?" — https://www.bahn.de/faq/zugbindung-aufgehoben-bedeutung*

**Working translation:** within Germany no additional certificate is needed for the lifted train binding; one can be issued on explicit request, for example on the train, at a travel centre or at the DB information desk.

The forecast itself disappears once the trains move on. A screenshot of the app or of bahn.de at the moment of the announcement records what DB showed, and that is exactly what clause 9.1.4 turns on.

## 3. Which part of the trip the ticket then covers

What decides is when the disruption is first announced:

- **Before departure:** the whole trip can be taken on another day, according to DB up to a year later.
- **On the way:** the ticket is valid from the station where the traveller gets off to the destination, right away or on a later day.
- **A part already travelled** is not travelled again on the same ticket.

Clause 9.1.1 grants the "continuation of the journey or onward travel … to the destination station". The EU rail passenger rights regulation it rests on uses the same words and names departure explicitly as one of the moments the expectation can arise:

> Muss entweder bei der Abfahrt oder im Falle eines verpassten Anschlusses oder eines Zugausfalls vernünftigerweise davon ausgegangen werden, dass bei Ankunft am Zielort gemäß Beförderungsvertrag die Verspätung 60 Minuten oder mehr betragen wird, so bietet das Eisenbahnunternehmen, das den verspäteten oder ausfallenden Dienst betreibt, dem Fahrgast unverzüglich an, sich für eine der folgenden Möglichkeiten zu entscheiden […] b) Fortsetzung der Fahrt oder Weiterreise mit geänderter Streckenführung unter vergleichbaren Beförderungsbedingungen bis zum Zielort bei nächster Gelegenheit; c) Fortsetzung der Fahrt oder Weiterreise mit geänderter Streckenführung unter vergleichbaren Beförderungsbedingungen bis zum Zielort zu einem späteren Zeitpunkt nach Wahl des Fahrgasts.
>
> *— Regulation (EU) 2021/782, Art. 18(1), German version — https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/DE/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32021R0782*

**Working translation:** where it must reasonably be expected, either at departure or in the event of a missed connection or a cancellation, that the delay in arrival at the final destination under the transport contract will be 60 minutes or more, the railway undertaking operating the delayed or cancelled service shall immediately offer the passenger the choice between […] (b) continuation or re-routing, under comparable transport conditions, to the final destination at the earliest opportunity; (c) continuation or re-routing, under comparable transport conditions, to the final destination at a later date at the passenger's convenience. (The official English text is at https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32021R0782.)

Only DB's help page names a time frame; the conditions of carriage themselves set none:

> Sie können eine spätere Verbindung Ihrer Wahl nutzen (z.B. später am selben Reisetag, einen Tag später oder auch erst in 3 Wochen. Dies ist bis zu einem Jahr nach ursprünglichem Reisedatum möglich).
>
> *— bahn.de, „In welchen Fällen ist die Zugbindung aufgehoben und was bedeutet das?" — https://www.bahn.de/faq/zugbindung-aufgehoben-bedeutung*

**Working translation:** a later connection of the traveller's choice may be used, later the same day, a day later or three weeks later, up to one year after the original travel date.

A journey is continued from where it stands. A single-journey ticket is one contract of carriage under clause 1.3.4 (https://www.bahn.de/agb), and only clause 9.1.3 leads back to the starting point. It applies only from an expected delay of 60 minutes, and then the journey is abandoned and the fare refunded, "zusammen mit einer Rückfahrt zum ersten Ausgangspunkt" where needed, together with a return trip to the first point of departure (https://www.bahn.de/agb).

| First announcement of the disruption | What the ticket then covers |
|---|---|
| before departure, the journey is not started | the whole trip, on another day, up to a year |
| on the way, the traveller gets off at a station | from that station to the destination, right away or up to a year later |
| only after departure, the journey was not started for another reason | not freed (section 4) |
| never announced, the journey travelled just arrives late | used; compensation only from 60 minutes |

One example is a Super Sparpreis from Aachen to Hannover with changes in Cologne and Hamm. If the app already shows in Aachen that the change in Hamm will fail, the whole journey can take place on another day. If it shows this only on arrival in Cologne, the ticket is valid from Cologne, also on a later day, and the Aachen–Cologne leg counts as travelled.

Neither of the first two rows is stated outright anywhere. The help page does not say from where the "later connection" runs, and only the regulation names the case before the journey starts, with "bei der Abfahrt" (at departure). Both rows are the obvious reading of the wording: a traveller still at the starting station continues from there. Whether a journey may be broken off on the way and the rest travelled on another day is the less certain of the two questions.

## 4. A journey that is never started

- **Not starting the journey is fine when the disruption was already announced before departure.** Then the whole trip is freed (section 3). Strictly speaking this holds only when the disruption is the reason, not, say, an illness.
- **What does not work is not starting the journey and relying on a disruption announced only once the journey was already under way.** The traveller would then have had to be on the train, and only the rest from the station of getting off would have been freed.

Where the conditions of carriage regulate not starting a journey at all, the delay is the reason: under clause 9.1.3 the journey may be abandoned or not started „aufgrund einer Verspätung", because of a delay, and clause 2.5.3 extends a ticket's validity when the traveller „infolge Verspätung oder Ausfall eines Zuges die Fahrt nicht antreten kann", cannot start the journey because of a delayed or cancelled train (https://www.bahn.de/agb).

The case in between is open: the disruption was announced before departure, but the journey was not started for a different reason. Clause 9.1.4 requires the announcement, not that it was read, and the help page speaks of a binding lifted „automatisch“, automatically. Whether that is enough depends in practice mostly on whether the announcement can be proven.

As in [section 2](#2-the-forecast-counts-not-how-the-journey-turns-out), what counts here too is the announcement at the time of departure, not how things turned out: a connection that actually arrives late in the evening does not free a ticket after the fact.

## 5. Limits

- **Reservation-compulsory trains and special trains are excluded.** Clause 9.1.1 rules them out expressly (quote in section 1).
- **Not every alternative offered is covered by the ticket.** That is the point of the sentence „Bitte prüfen Sie vorher, ob Ihr Ticket für die ausgewählte Alternative gültig ist" (check beforehand whether the ticket is valid for the alternative) in the Navigator help (quote in section 2). Contracts with operators that do not apply the conditions of carriage are provided for in clause 9.1.2 only where DB fails to inform about onward travel options within 100 minutes (https://www.bahn.de/agb).
- **A Deutschland-Ticket plus a long-distance ticket are two contracts.** If the regional train misses the long-distance train, the long-distance ticket keeps its train binding (https://www.bahn.de/faq/deutschlandticket-kombi-fv-dt).
- **Regional tickets work differently.** The help page: „Bei Nutzung eines höherwertigen Zugs (z.B. IC/EC, ICE) müssen Sie ein zusätzliches Ticket kaufen. Die Kosten können Sie über das Servicecenter Fahrgastrechte erstatten lassen. Diese Regelung gilt nicht für erheblich ermäßigte Fahrkarten (z.B. Deutschland-Ticket, Länder-Tickets)." (*Working translation:* a higher-class train such as an IC/EC or ICE requires an additional ticket, whose cost can be reclaimed from the passenger-rights service centre; this does not apply to heavily discounted tickets such as the Deutschland-Ticket or regional state tickets.) (https://www.bahn.de/faq/zugbindung-aufgehoben-bedeutung)

## 6. Assessment

- The assessment rests on the wording of the conditions of carriage, the regulation and the help pages quoted. No published case law on these situations is apparent.
- The conditions of carriage are reissued several times a year. The edition quoted is that of 26 October 2026; the numbering may shift in later editions.
- The EU regulation ties onward travel to an expected delay of 60 minutes (quote in section 3). The 20-minute threshold for journeys within Germany is a contractual rule of DB and goes beyond it.
