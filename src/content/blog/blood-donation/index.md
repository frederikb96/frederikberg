In short

- **The cost is real, small and mostly short.** Peak endurance drops by a few per cent and is back after about two weeks. Easy training is hardly affected.
- **No sign of more infections.** In the largest study, iron deficiency among male donors was not linked to more infections.
- **Iron is the slow part.** Refilling the iron stores takes months. For regular donors, a ferritin value says more than the haemoglobin test before each donation.

A standard whole-blood donation takes about half a litre. The Stanford Blood Center puts that against the whole body: "The average adult has 10 pints of blood in their bodies, and whole blood donations are currently capped at one pint per person" [\[1\]](https://stanfordbloodcenter.org/pulse-spring23-what-really-happens-to-your-body-after-you-donate-blood-effects-risks-and-recovery/). This article answers four questions about that tenth:

- How quickly does the body recover, and what does it do to sport?
- Does a donation make getting ill more likely?
- Which part takes longest to come back?
- When does a donation have to wait?

## 1. Circulation and endurance

The body replaces the fluid within days and the red cells within weeks, and performance follows the red cells:

- **Plasma is back within a few days.** It "will begin to replenish immediately and should be fully back to normal within a few days", while "it typically takes 4–8 weeks to replace all the red cells lost in a whole blood donation" [\[1\]](https://stanfordbloodcenter.org/pulse-spring23-what-really-happens-to-your-body-after-you-donate-blood-effects-risks-and-recovery/). Drinking enough speeds up the first part, since plasma is mostly water.
- **Peak endurance drops by a few per cent.** One to two days after a donation, maximal oxygen uptake is about 7 % lower and maximal exercise capacity about 10 % lower [\[2\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27807869/).
- **It is back after about two weeks.** Easy endurance training is hardly affected.

The first figures come from a review of 18 before-after studies. Its authors rate the underlying studies as "of low quality" and describe the effects as "small but potentially physiologically important reductions [...] in the first 2 days after the blood donation" [\[2\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27807869/). A Danish study of 19 healthy men shows the time course: peak oxygen uptake fell by 6.5 % and a time trial was 5.2 % slower on day 3, and "Both VO2peak and TT performance were back to baseline 14 days after blood donation", while haemoglobin took longer [\[3\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25512178/). A randomised trial of repeated donations concluded that "Maximal, but not submaximal, endurance capacity was altered after blood donation" [\[4\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27873242/). Hard intervals and races in the two weeks after a donation are therefore affected; a relaxed run is not.

No study measuring strength after a donation was found. Short efforts depend little on oxygen transport, so a large effect would be surprising, but that is reasoning, not evidence.

## 2. Infection risk

The worry of getting ill after donating finds no support in the largest data set on the question:

- **No link for men.** The Danish Blood Donor Study followed 94,628 donors. Iron deficiency "was not associated with overall risk of infection for men"; in women it went with slightly more antibiotic prescriptions [\[5\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39807019/).
- **The authors' own conclusion:** "risk of infection should not be considered an apprehension regarding blood donation" [\[5\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39807019/).
- **A gap remains.** That study looks at iron deficiency among donors, not at the days right after a donation, and no large study of that narrower window was found.

The honest result is therefore "no sign of a risk" rather than "risk ruled out".

## 3. Iron is the slow part

Iron is the one thing a donation removes that takes months to come back:

- **Months, not days.** In a randomised trial with 215 donors, "Without iron supplements, 67% of participants did not recover iron stores by 168 days" [\[6\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25668261/). One low-dose iron tablet a day shortened the recovery of most of the lost haemoglobin to about a month.
- **Ferritin says more than haemoglobin.** The haemoglobin test before every donation only drops once the stores are already low. Ferritin shows the stores themselves, and a study of male donors recommends it as "a more adequate index to use for iron deficiency screening" [\[7\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20859513/).

For someone donating once or twice a year this is a small risk, and asking for a ferritin value now and then settles it.

Reported health benefits of donating, such as lower blood pressure or fewer heart attacks, are no good reason to donate. Donors tend to be healthier before they ever donate: in a study of 0.6 million people in China, non-donors had a 15 % higher rate of the studied diseases than matched donors before the first donation [\[8\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38386403/). The reason to donate is the recipient.

## 4. When a donation has to wait (Germany)

German blood services defer donors under common rules; the final decision is made by the doctor on site. In short:

- **After a cold or another uncomplicated infection:** one week.
- **After fever or diarrhoea of unclear cause:** four weeks.
- **After most vaccinations, such as flu, tetanus, tick-borne encephalitis or COVID-19:** no wait, as long as there are no symptoms. Live vaccines and the hepatitis B vaccine: four weeks.

The deferral list of the blood donation service at Uniklinik Aachen, as one example, reads:

> nach fieberhaften Erkrankungen und/oder Durchfallerkrankungen unklarer Ursache für 4 Wochen, [...] nach einem unkomplizierten Infekt für 1 Woche.
>
> *— Uniklinik RWTH Aachen, Blutspendedienst, Ausschlussgründe von einer Blutspende [\[9\]](https://www.ukaachen.de/fileadmin/files/institute/transfusionsmedizin/Blutspendedienst/20250926_DM_Dokument_Rueckstellungsgruende_WEB.pdf)*

(After illnesses with fever and/or diarrhoea of unclear cause, four weeks; after an uncomplicated infection, one week.)

> Keine Rückstellung ist erforderlich nach Applikation von Tot- bzw. Toxoidimpfstoffen oder gentechnisch hergestellten Impfstoffen (zum Beispiel Poliomyelitis inaktiviert, Typhus inaktiviert, Diphtherie, Influenza, Cholera inaktiviert, Tetanus, FSME, Hepatitis A, SARS-CoV-2), sofern der Spender ohne klinische Symptome und bei Wohlbefinden ist.
>
> *— same document [\[9\]](https://www.ukaachen.de/fileadmin/files/institute/transfusionsmedizin/Blutspendedienst/20250926_DM_Dokument_Rueckstellungsgruende_WEB.pdf)*

(No deferral after inactivated, toxoid or recombinant vaccines, for example influenza, tetanus, tick-borne encephalitis, hepatitis A or SARS-CoV-2, as long as the donor has no symptoms and feels well.)

## 5. Assessment

A healthy person can donate without a lasting cost. The practical consequences are small: a quiet donation day with plenty to drink, no hard endurance session for a few days and no important endurance event in the two weeks after, and, for regular donors, a look at ferritin rather than haemoglobin alone.
